Every September I sit down with Alder Valley's planting list and work out
what we'll be cooking until Christmas. This year it took two coffees and
most of a Monday.

## What's new

**Roast squash with labneh and dukkah.** The farm grew three kinds of
squash this year and we're using all of them. They roast slowly in the
morning while the bread is in, and we serve them warm with our own labneh
and a dukkah that Teo keeps adjusting. The flatbread is from the same
dough as the sourdough, rolled thin and charred.

**Leek and Gruyère tart.** Leeks from the farm, sweated down for an hour
with butter and thyme. The pastry is the one we use for the rhubarb
tarts, which means it's very short and slightly difficult to cut. We've
decided that's a feature.

**Cardamom porridge with poached quince.** The quince tree behind
Gull Rock Dairy has outdone itself. We poach them in a light syrup with
bay and serve them on porridge cooked in oat milk, so it's vegan without
anyone noticing.

## What's staying

The brown-butter eggs, obviously. The mackerel, as long as the boats
are bringing it in. And the fish finger sandwich, which I tried to take
off the menu once in 2021 and won't try again.

## What's gone until next year

The tomato toast and the crab salad. The crab on toast stays — the crab
is good until well into winter — but the salad was a summer thing.

## And the rhubarb

Forced rhubarb doesn't really start until January, but the farm has a
patch under the old cold frames that comes up early. So the rhubarb and
custard tart is back on the counter from this week, a few months ahead
of schedule. It won't last, so if you see it, have it.

See you at the counter.
