People ask why the fish on our menu changes from one day to the next. The
honest answer is that we buy whatever the day boats bring in, and the day
boats bring in whatever the sea lets them.

There are four boats that still fish out of Port Alder every day the
weather allows. They go out before five and come back between nine and
eleven, and they sell straight off the quay — to us, to the fish van, and
to anyone who turns up with a bag.

## Half past four

I went out with Ellis on the *Morwenna* last week, which I try to do once
a summer to remind myself what the fish costs in effort. We were past the
headland by five. The mackerel were feeding near the surface, so it was
handlines: a string of feathers, drop, wait, pull. Ellis does it without
looking. I caught eleven and felt very pleased with myself; he caught
about two hundred.

## Back on the quay

We were in by half nine. The crab pots had been lifted the day before and
the crabs were waiting in the keep. I bought forty mackerel, six crabs
and a box of pollack, and was back in the kitchen before the first lunch
order.

That mackerel was on the grill by twelve. That's the whole point.

## What this means for the menu

- **Mackerel** is on most days from June to October. If the boats haven't
  gone out, it isn't.
- **Crab** comes in most weeks from spring until the first proper storms.
- **The fish in the fish finger sandwich** is whatever white fish the
  boats had: pollack, coley or whiting. We don't use cod.
- When the weather keeps the boats in, the board says so, and we cook
  something else.

If you're ever up early, walk down to the quay at half nine and watch
them come in. Then come and have breakfast.
