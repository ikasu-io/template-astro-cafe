I get asked about the bun more than anything else, so here it is.

## Where it comes from

My grandmother was from Gothenburg. Every Saturday she made a tray of
kardemummabullar, and I spent most of my childhood trying to eat them
before they cooled. When we opened, I wanted one thing on the counter
that was ours and not just a good version of something everybody makes.
It was always going to be the bun.

## What goes in

- A soft enriched dough with butter, milk and a little sugar.
- Green cardamom, cracked fresh every morning. Pre-ground cardamom tastes
  of dust after a week; it's the single biggest difference.
- A filling of soft butter, sugar and more cardamom.
- Pearl sugar on top and a quick brush of syrup as they come out.

## Why the twist

You cut the rolled dough into strips, stretch each one, twist it and tie
it into a knot. It's fiddly, and it takes a few hundred to get right. But
the twist makes layers, and the layers are why the edges are crisp and
the middle stays soft. A plain roll is a perfectly nice bun. It isn't
this one.

## Why they're gone by eleven

Because I bake one batch, at six, and I'm not getting up at four for a
second one. We make about a hundred and twenty. On a Saturday in August
that's not enough, and I've made my peace with it.
